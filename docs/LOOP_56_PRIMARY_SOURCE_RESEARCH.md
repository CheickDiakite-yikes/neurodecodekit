## Primary-Source Findings

### 1. EEG and MEG measure related activity through different physical signals

Brain2Qwerty v1 describes EEG as recording electric fields and MEG as recording
magnetic fields generated by cortical activity. Its specific devices also
differ: 61 EEG plus three ocular channels versus 102 magnetometers plus 204
planar gradiometers.

Source: [Noninvasive decoding of typed sentences from human brain
activity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02303-2)

Decision: the modalities can share task and reporting vocabulary, but not
channel identity, units, reference rules, geometry, sensor transforms, or
calibration assumptions.

### 2. Published performance is conditional on a complete experimental system

Brain2Qwerty v1 reports 29% mean MEG CER and 65% mean EEG CER under the same
published model family, but it uses modality-specific recordings, subjects,
sensor arrays, and observed keypress-aligned windows. Its language model also
improves some sentence metrics while using linguistic regularities.

Source: [Brain2Qwerty v1 results and
methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02303-2)

Decision: those numbers may be quoted only with their external cohort and
method identity. They cannot define a local pass threshold, be pooled with
NeuroDecodeKit scores, or imply that reducing MEG channel count predicts EEG
performance.

### 3. Continuous input is not causal real-time output

The official Brain2Qwerty v2 page says v2 removes the need for individual
keypress timing and generates sentences from continuous MEG. The paper also
states that the architecture is noncausal, works with an entire sentence, and
cannot show words before the sentence ends.

Sources: [Brain2Qwerty v2 project
page](https://facebookresearch.github.io/brain2qwerty/) and [Brain2Qwerty v2
paper](https://facebookresearch.github.io/brain2qwerty/assets/brain2qwerty_v2.pdf)

Decision: Loop 56 separates continuous input, asynchronous sentence output,
causal incremental output, and measured end-to-end latency. None may be used as
a synonym for another.

### 4. Brain2Qwerty v2 is cryogenic MEG evidence

The v2 paper reports nine healthy volunteers, ten roughly one-hour sessions
per participant, and a 306-channel MEGIN system with 102 magnetometers and 204
planar gradiometers. It says the scanner remains difficult to adapt clinically
and presents OPM-MEG as future research.

Source: [Brain2Qwerty v2
paper](https://facebookresearch.github.io/brain2qwerty/assets/brain2qwerty_v2.pdf)

Decision: v2 is not EEG, OPM-MEG, portable hardware, home use, or patient
evidence. Random ablation of cryogenic MEG sensors remains a model sensitivity
study on that acquisition system.

### 5. EEG reference is part of the recorded signal definition

BIDS distinguishes an EEG electrode from a recorded channel and requires the
EEG reference scheme. It also requires sampling frequency and preserves
channel-specific reference, units, filters, and channel order.

Source: [BIDS EEG specification
1.11.1](https://bids-specification.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modality-specific-files/electroencephalography.html)

Decision: an EEG array is not numerically or physically defined by channel
names and count alone. Reference, ground, gain, units, electrode roles, contact,
and derived rereference operations remain modality-specific evidence.

### 6. MEG sensor classes are not interchangeable channels

BIDS assigns separate channel types to magnetometers, axial gradiometers,
planar gradiometers, and MEG reference sensors, alongside EOG, EMG, trigger,
audio, gaze, and localization channels.

Source: [BIDS MEG specification
1.11.1](https://bids-specification.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modality-specific-files/magnetoencephalography.html)

Decision: channel count, order, geometry, orientation, compensation, projector,
and reference-sensor roles must stay explicit. A shared tensor shape cannot
establish a shared sensor representation.

### 7. A compact home-device specification is mechanics evidence only

OpenBCI documents Cyton packets as one counter, eight 24-bit channel values,
six auxiliary bytes, and one footer at 250 Hz. That describes transport and
numeric payload, not electrode placement, reference correctness, signal
quality, task information, or decoding.

Source: [OpenBCI Cyton data
format](https://docs.openbci.com/Cyton/CytonDataFormat/)

Decision: a future eight-channel local stream may share a packet-to-cache
interface with research EEG, but it cannot inherit the S20 or published 61-EEG-
channel scientific result.

## Five Verdict Classes

Every Loop 56 matrix cell must use exactly one class:

| Class | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `L56-X0` | shared proven artifact | the exact same committed artifact or dependency-free algorithm is valid for both modalities |

algorithm; a CER value cannot. A cache schema can be shared; a cached signal,
channel ontology, or learned representation cannot.

## Accessibility Is A Conjunction

An at-home claim needs all of the following for one named device and protocol:

- device presence and exact firmware/software identity;
- verified EEG electrode, reference, ground, gain, and channel roles;
- local raw access without mandatory cloud transport;
- measured packet, clock, loss, reconnect, and retention behavior;
- target-blind signal quality and artifact inventory;
- repeated self-application with setup time and failure rate;
- task and target integrity;
- a sensor-signal effect over no-signal and peripheral controls;
- causal incremental parity;
- measured capture-to-visible-output latency;
- privacy, deletion, consent, and license compliance; and
- safety and usability evidence for the target population.

OpenBCI specification evidence currently satisfies none of those as a complete
conjunction. It provides candidate mechanics fields only.
